The Quilting Process

It is important to note that every quilter has their own preferred method of sewing a quilt. The process listed showcases one method, but you may find others through various sources.

To begin, a pattern must be chosen to plan how the final quilt will look. These can be found through various sources, such as online websites, books, or by creating one. By finding patterns online or in books, instructions will also be provided. They will provide information on sizes to cut the fabric, how to sew each piece together, and other necessary instructions to complete that pattern. After solidifying a pattern, fabric can be selected. Usually, a pattern is chosen before the fabric to ensure the best fabric is selected to match the desired outcome.

Once the pattern and fabric choices are solidified, the ironing and cutting process can begin. It is essential to iron fabric throughout the process, especially before cutting it. Make sure that each piece is flat and ironed before cutting, as this can not be undone.

After ironing and cutting the fabric, the sewing process begins. Follow your chosen pattern to know how to sew each piece together. After sewing each piece, iron down the seams to ensure pieces stay flat as you continue sewing more together.

Sewing each piece together can be a long process, but once complete, this part of the quilt will be the final quilt top. The batting and backing fabric will now be cut to the same size as the quilt top. When these pieces are combined, this is commonly known as a quilt sandwich. It can be kept together by pins or clips. If a long-arm machine is accessible, a quilter will take the quilt sandwich to one to have the stitching pattern sewn on. An alternative to using a long-arm is sewing straight lines using a regular machine. Either method will serve the purpose of keeping the final quilt layers together.

The final step in the quilting process is to add the binding. This piece will conceal the raw edges of the quilt, creating a finished look. One can buy binding strips or create their own by cutting long strips of fabric. The binding is partially or fully machine-sewn on. One can hand-sew part of the binding to hide the seams, but machine-sewing can be quicker.

Whether a large quilt is being made or just a wall hanging, this method is used in most cases. Throughout the process, it is important to be patient and set aside the time needed for the best possible result. After completing a quilt, enjoy admiring it as a decoration or wrapping up in a new, cozy blanket.
