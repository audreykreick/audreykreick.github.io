Quilting Advice

Sometimes the smallest pieces of advice make the biggest difference! This page is a collection of practical, everyday tips and tricks to help make your quilting experience smoother and more enjoyable. These are the kinds of things that don't always make it into formal tutorials, but that every beginner should know before diving in. From pressing your seams correctly to keeping your cuts accurate, the tips below will help you work more efficiently, avoid common frustrations, and get better results from the very start!

Your iron is more important than you think

    Before you even begin sewing, make sure you iron all your fabric. Once ironed, your cuts will be straighter and your stitches will be more accurate. After sewing pieces together, you should also iron your seams so that your next stitch is equally as straight. Honestly, I think I spend more time at my ironing board than at my sewing table!

Don't forget to change your needle

    To ensure smooth sewing, it is important that your needle isn't dull. Its recommended that after every project you should change it out. Realistically, I probably change mine after every few projects or when I notice it may be time for a replacement. A cheap pack of needles from Amazon will usually do just fine, just make sure they are compatible with your machine. You can also look up YouTube videos for your specific machine to see how to change the needle.

Save time with chain piecing

    When sewing a bunch of tiny squares or shapes, chain piecing should be your go to. Instead of taking the fabric off after every seam, you can stitch multiple fabric pieces together in a continuous line without stopping or cutting the thread in between. After you finish sewing all the pieces, you can cut them apart and save tons of time!

Double check before you cut fabric

    There have been so many times where I've accidentally cut more than I needed to and ended up wasting fabric. While this may sound simple, make sure to double check your measurements before making the final cut!

Save your scraps

    When working on all your projects, you will most likely have some scrap pieces left. I always save these pieces because they can be great for future projects that require smaller pieces. There are also a ton of patterns that are designed to be made for scraps to reduce waste. I have a small bin where I keep scrap pieces, and I highly recommend using one!

Always buy extra fabric

    In order to account for cutting or stitching mistakes, you should always buy some extra fabric. (Especially if you thrift fabric). It doesn't need to be a lot, but just enough to help you through any mistakes. Take it from me - you don't want to have to pause a project to get more fabric from a simple mistake. If you end up with extra, you can always use it for another small project!

Sew on scrap fabric before your project

    Before I ever begin sewing, I always test my machine by sewing on a scrap piece of fabric. This way, I can ensure my bobbin is correct, the tension is accurate, and everything is working as it should. Its much better to make a mistake on a scrap piece than your actual project! But, if you forget this step, you can always grab your seam ripper.

Use a sewing marker for straight lines

    When I quilt my own projects, I always use a sewing marker to draw straight lines with my ruler as a guide for when I sew. This way, your finished project can look clean and be free of mistakes! Make sure you use a marker specific for quilting so that it washes away with water.
