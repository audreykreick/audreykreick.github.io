Where to Buy Your Supplies

Shopping in person for fabrics and threads is one of the most exciting parts of making a quilt or sewing project! This is where your idea or pattern can really come to life. While there are many local quilt shops, this list also consists of larger chains that you can definitely find near you. I have shopped at all of these places and they all offer budget friendly supplies that can get you through your first project.

Local Quilt Store

I would highly recommend searching for small quilting shops near you. They will have a wider selection of patterned fabrics and high quality threads. The people that work there can also give you some helpful advice for creating your first project! You might also see their quilts hung all around the walls. These places likely have a long-armer that can complete that part of the quilt for you. This is the decorative stitching that you see on quilts. This usually costs $50-$200 depending on the size of your quilt. For small projects, you can just use your basic machine for a simple quilted look.

Local Thrift Store

Most thrift stores have a craft section that is filled with vintage and unique fabrics. This is a super cost effective option that I personally do all of the time. Some of my favorite fabrics have been thrifted! While thrifted fabrics are a great option, I wouldn't recommend thrifting thread. Older threads can be weaker and break easily. Make sure to wash your thrifted fabric before using it!

Hobby Lobby

Out of all the big craft stores, Hobby Lobby is my favorite. They always have a huge selection! If you run out of a specific fabric you previously bought, there is probably more available. They also have no shortage of threads, batting, and everything else you'd need for a project. Similar to a local thrift shop, the people that work there would also be happy to help you navigate the quilting process.

Michael's

After Joann's closed down, their fabric selection moved to Michael's. In my experience, the fabric here is very limited, but you'll be able to find something if its your only option. In addition to fabric, they have good options for thread, batting, and other sewing supplies. They sell their fabric in precut packages, so it is a quicker process than going to Hobby Lobby or a local store. Overall a good option, especially if it is close to you!

Walmart

Though it may not seem like it, Walmart is an amazing option for sewing supplies. The quality is not as good as other places, but it is a great budget-friendly option. I often shop here for smaller projects or if I need to quickly get some fabric in a solid color. They also sell other sewing supplies like Hobby Lobby and Michael's. If you're already grocery shopping and need some sewing supplies, Walmart is your go to.
